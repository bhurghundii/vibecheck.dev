Why vibecheck exists

25 Oct 2026

Thanks to AI, it is far easier to generate code that people just do not understand. The burden of understanding therefore falls on the reviewers.

When reviewers face a pile of massive PRs that nobody has the energy to read closely, the result is burnout and rubber-stamping:

"LGTM, CI is green."

Then you are shipping code that nobody understands. For business critical software, that is simply unacceptable.

What vibecheck does about it

vibecheck is a self-hosted merge gate for GitHub pull requests. Before a PR can merge, the person who opened it has to show they understand their own change.

  1. vibecheck reads the change, writes a few questions about it, and comments on the PR with a link.
  2. The author answers them in plain English, one question at a time. The first is always: how does this change work at a high level?
  3. Claude grades each answer against the actual change. You tune how much detail a pass needs.
  4. Pass, and vibecheck/verdict goes green. Fall short, and the merge stays blocked.

To be clear, vibecheck does not review the code for you. It just checks that the author understands it.

The irony

I am well aware of the irony of using vibe slop to deal with the problem of vibe slop. I actively encourage AI generated PRs to the project, because the entire point is to help developers integrate vibe coding more safely. I still think humans beat AI any day though.
